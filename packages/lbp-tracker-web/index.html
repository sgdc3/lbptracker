Compose on the game’s own grid

The Music Sequencer as an editor: a board of instruments, eight steps to a bar, and inside each one a note grid where a note is a chain of points — the size of a point its volume, its colour its timbre, and a straight line between two points the glide the engine plays. Every change is heard through the same engine as the renderer, at once. Everything happens on your own computer.

2 The board — four bars to a tile, one instrument per chip

0:00 / 0:00 idle

One row is selected at a time — click its number, a chip on it, or a cell — and as the song plays the grid below follows the chip the playhead is inside on that row. Drag across empty cells to draw a new chip as long as the drag — four bars at least, then two at a time — and pick its instrument when you let go; a double-click draws a four-bar one. Click a chip to edit its notes below; drag it to another cell. Click the bar numbers to seek; drag the board’s bottom-right corner to make it taller. Delete removes the selected chip when the board has the focus, Ctrl+D duplicates it into the cursor cell. A chip is as long as its note grid — four bars, then two more at a time — and chips may overlap in time, though the game’s composers rarely let them; the selected one is drawn on top.

3 The notes

no instrument selected

Click to place a note, drag to give it an end — sideways for a held note, diagonally for a glide. Drag a point to move it; Shift+drag up or down for its volume, Alt+drag for its timbre. Double-click a line to add a point, double-click a point to remove it; right-click removes a point or, on the line, the whole note. Shift+drag on empty space selects; Ctrl+click adds to the selection. Arrow keys nudge the selection, +/- its volume, [/] its timbre, Delete removes it, Ctrl+C/V copy and paste, Ctrl+Z/Y undo and redo, Space plays. The keyboard on the left plays the instrument.
