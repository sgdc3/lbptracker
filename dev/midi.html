Take the music somewhere else

Save a song out of one of your levels as a MIDI file and open it in whatever you write music in — or bring a MIDI file back the other way and hear it played by the game’s own instruments. The export is MPE, one channel per sounding note, because that is the only way a bend or a fade stays attached to the note it belongs to. Everything happens on your own computer.

how to write it

A track is named row 4 - saw_wave, because the board row and the instrument are the two things MIDI has no message for — so the name is where they live, and editing it is how you move a part. The same instrument can sit on one row several times with different level, pan or sends: those are separate placements to the game and get a #2. One track per row puts them together instead and writes the mixer as CC automation at each clip, which over the ten-level corpus is 4,911 tracks down to 3,224. Placements that are sounding at the same time keep their own track anyway — one track has one mixer state, and there is no honest way to give two sounding placements different pans on it. That happens to 2 row groups of 850, and it is what makes this free rather than a trade.

⚠ The last option is what makes a round trip exact. Everything a MIDI file can say is said in MIDI, but a handful of clips still come back cut into different records — a ramp re-simplified onto the staircase its own rounding makes, a note that fits two overlapping cells. None of it is audible and all of it is a different file, so the exporter reads its own output back and writes the originals verbatim for the clips that differ. Over the ten-level corpus that is 177 clips of 62,158, and it takes the record-level difference to zero. Turn it off if the file is going somewhere that will edit the notes: a patch describes records the edited notes no longer match.

MPE spends the sixteen channels on notes, so every glide and every fade belongs to one note. Plain spends them on instruments, which is what a General MIDI player expects — and a bend on a shared channel would move the whole part, so in that mode the glides are dropped rather than faked. The count below says how many.

⚠ A file’s tracks do not get a channel space each. The header can declare 65,535 of them and they still share one set of sixteen: the channel is in the status byte, not in the track.

A DAW puts them back, though, and this file is written for one that does. Reaper — and anything else that imports a file as one project track per MIDI track — gives each track its own instrument, and that instrument only ever sees its own track’s events. So every part gets all fifteen member channels: nothing shares across parts, nothing is dragged. Played instead as a single stream — a hardware module, a plain player — the parts would collide on the same channels. Split is the option for a destination that cannot promise separate tracks: separate files, packed as tightly as their polyphony allows, handed over as a zip.

bend range

auto

How far a full pitch bend reaches. MPE’s own default is ±48, and this corpus needs more: 582 of its 1,448,224 control points glide further, the widest by 62 semitones. Left on auto the file gets exactly the range its widest slide asks for, never below 48 and never above MIDI’s ceiling of 96.

    The imported song is played by the same engine as the other pages — the game’s samples, key splits, envelopes, filter and output stage. A file that came from here brings its instruments with it; one from a DAW has none, so pick what its parts should sound like.

    What survives the trip, and what cannot

    The grid. A step is a sixteenth and a note can sit a third of one late, so the file is written at 480 ticks per quarter: a step is 120 ticks and a third of one is exactly 40. Nothing is rounded. The older converter used 96 and could not hold a triplet at all — its fourth slot landed on the next beat, which is why triplet passages came out of it sounding swung.

    The glides. A note in this sequencer is a chain of control points and the engine ramps between them; 9.3% of the corpus’s notes do. MIDI pitch bend holds its value until the next message, so a glide written as two events would be a jump — the export resamples it onto the same third-of-a-step grid the records use, and the import folds the samples back to the points they came from.

    What MIDI has no room for rides in text meta events: the tempo and swing, the mixer’s NumChannels and volumes, and per part its instrument GUID, row, level, pan and sends. A file that goes out and comes back is the same song.

    What it cannot carry. An MPE zone has fifteen member channels and this engine has thirty-two voices, so a dense passage runs out. A note that has to share writes no bend and no pressure — those belong to the channel, and a newcomer setting them would drag whatever is already sounding there with it — so it loses its own glide instead. The notes that carry a glide are allocated first for that reason, and a note with nothing to express is parked on the master channel rather than taking one from a glide. Across the corpus that leaves 0.16% of notes short of a glide, and the count is always shown rather than swallowed. Key and Scale are folded into the note numbers on the way out, because a MIDI file has to play in something that has never heard of an LBP scale; an import therefore comes back chromatic, sounding the same and written differently.
